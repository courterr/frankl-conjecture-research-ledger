PROBLEM
Let F be a finite family of distinct finite sets, containing a nonempty set, closed under pairwise union. Must there be an element x for which f(x) >= |F|/2, where f(x) is the number of members containing x?
Take U to be the union of the members, and m=|F|.

MATHEMATICAL OUTPUT 1 — ORDINARY AVERAGING DOES NOT SUFFICE
The incidence identity is
  sum over x in U of f(x) = sum over A in F of |A|.
If the right-hand side is at least |U|m/2, an abundant element follows immediately.

But that sufficient condition is not universal. For
  F = {empty set, {a}, {b}, {a,b}, {a,b,c}},
all unions stay in F. Total set size is 7, below |U|m/2 = 7.5.
The frequencies are f(a)=f(b)=3 and f(c)=1.
The conjecture holds, while average element frequency is below half.
Outcome: a valid sufficient condition, with an explicit obstacle to treating it as the full proof.
Novelty: no novelty claimed; ordinary counting and this type of counterexample are already familiar.

MATHEMATICAL OUTPUT 2 — FIXED-MEMBER FIBRE DECOMPOSITION
Choose a nonempty S in F, with k=|S|.
For each T contained in U minus S, define
  H_T = {R contained in S : T union R belongs to F}.
Ignore empty H_T.

Facts:
(a) Every nonempty H_T contains S.
Proof: if T union R is in F, its union with the family member S is T union S, also in F.
(b) Each H_T is union-closed.
Proof: unioning T union R and T union R' produces T union (R union R').
(c) The fibres partition F by the part outside S.
(d) Different fibres satisfy an additional compatibility condition:
  R in H_T and Q in H_V imply R union Q in H_(T union V).

Define the signed incidence balance
  D(H_T) = sum over R in H_T of (2|R|-k).
Then
  sum over T of D(H_T) = 2 sum over x in S of f(x) - k m.
If that sum is nonnegative, some x in S is abundant.
These are elementary identities, not a claimed new reduction or solution.

MATHEMATICAL OUTPUT 3 — COMPLETE PROOF OF A RESTRICTED CASE
Claim: If F contains a member S of size one or two, then some element of S occurs in at least half of F.

For k=1, the possible traces have contributions -1 for the empty trace and +1 for S. Each nonempty fibre contains S; its balance is therefore nonnegative.

For k=2, the empty trace contributes -2, each singleton contributes 0, and S contributes +2.
Each nonempty fibre contains S, and the empty trace can appear only once. Its balance is again nonnegative.

Summing over all nonempty fibres gives
  2 sum over x in S of f(x) >= k m.
At least one x in S consequently satisfies f(x) >= m/2.
This proof covers arbitrary family size, subject to the one- or two-element member condition.

Outcome: proved special case, known in the existing line of local-configuration arguments. It is baseline progress within this attempt, not new mathematics.

MATHEMATICAL OUTPUT 4 — THE STRONGER FIBRE CLAIM IS FALSE
Candidate generalization:
  Every union-closed family H of subsets of S containing S has D(H)>=0.
This would extend the preceding fibre-by-fibre proof to all k.

Counterexample for k=3:
  S={a,b,c},
  H={empty set, {a}, {b}, {a,b}, {a,b,c}}.
This H is union-closed and contains S, but its contributions are
  -3, -1, -1, +1, +3,
whose sum is -1.

Thus nonnegative balance in every individual fibre cannot be assumed beyond the established small cases. This is a counterexample to the proposed strengthening, not to Frankl's conjecture: a and b remain abundant.
The example was constructed and checked by direct inspection in the reasoning attempt, not found by a search program.

MATHEMATICAL OUTPUT 5 — WHY TWO OTHER STANDARD ROUTES REMAIN INCOMPLETE
Injection:
For fixed x, split F into F0 (sets lacking x) and F1 (sets containing x). An injection F0 -> F1 would prove abundance of x. If {x} is a member, the map A -> A union {x} works. For larger S containing x, A -> A union S can identify distinct inputs. Example: with S={a,b,c}, both {a} and {b} map to S in the family above. No general repair was established.

Deletion/induction:
Deleting a coordinate can merge two distinct original sets. An ordinary induction hypothesis on the projected family counts each image once, while the original family effectively gives images multiplicity one or two. A frequency guarantee in the unweighted projection does not automatically transfer. No general transfer theorem was established.

Outcome: identified gaps, not proofs that every injection or induction strategy is impossible.
