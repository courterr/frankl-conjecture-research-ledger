CANDIDATE RECORDED BEFORE LITERATURE SEARCH
Seed P097, William Blake, Spring: several changing voices produce a repeated refrain; muting one voice reveals its contribution. Mathematical translation: count minimal unions of irreducible generators, crediting a coordinate only when exactly one generator supplies it.
For a finite union-closed F containing empty, take its nonempty join-irreducible members J. For each nonempty A in F, enumerate every inclusion-minimal subfamily D of J whose union is A. For each x in A, award one unit when x belongs to exactly one generator of D. Sum these units over all A and all such D to obtain W(x), the private-witness count.
Testable selector claim: at least one coordinate maximizing W(x) is abundant in F. This is a proposed sufficient selector, not a theorem, and no direct causal advantage of poetry is asserted. The generator/irredundance framework is familiar; the exact selector needs a literature check before being called novel. It also risks weighting a member many times according to its number of decompositions.


EXACT DEFINITIONS
Assume F contains empty and a nonempty member. Let J be the nonempty members that cannot be written as unions of other members. These are the irreducible generators. Every nonempty member has a representation as a union of a subfamily of J.
A decomposition D subset J of A is minimal when union D=A and removing any selected generator changes the union. In such a decomposition, every selected generator has some private coordinate, belonging to it and no other selected generator.
For each coordinate x define W(x) as the number of pairs (A,D), where D is a minimal decomposition of A, in which x belongs to exactly one generator in D.
The proposed rule selects a coordinate maximizing W. We explicitly allowed ties: the claim was only that at least one maximizer must be abundant. The counterexample has a unique maximizer, so it refutes even this tie-permissive version.

SEARCH AND COUNTEREXAMPLE
The rule survived all2479 nontrivial union-closed families containing empty on four available labelled ground points. A deterministic random search on five to seven points then found a counterexample at the615th checked random family. This is not a success rate, representative sample, or proof of a size threshold.
The first example had27 members on seven points. Greedy generator deletion and coordinate projection reduced it to18 members on six points. This is a convenient reduced example, not a claim of global minimality.

Let the six coordinates be a,b,c,d,e,f. Use generators
 {b,c}, {a,e}, {a,c,e}, {b,c,e}, {d,f}, {e,f}.
Their union-closure, with empty included, consists exactly of
 empty, bc, ae, ace, bce, abce, df, bcdf, ef,
 aef, acef, bcef, abcef, def, adef, acdef, bcdef, abcdef.
Here a string such as bc denotes the set {b,c}.

Coordinate                a   b   c   d   e   f
Ordinary frequency        9   8  11   7  14  12
Private-witness score W  18  19  16  11  14  18

The unique W-maximizer is b, yet f(b)=8<18/2=9. Several other coordinates are abundant, so Frankl holds for this family.

WHY THE RULE FAILS
The score counts decompositions, while abundance counts distinct members. The member abce has four minimal decompositions, and b is private in all four. The members abcef and abcdef each also give b four private credits. These three members alone contribute12 credits for b. Repeated representations of the same target can therefore dominate the selector. Private importance within a representation is not a frequency certificate across distinct sets.
There are28 minimal decompositions of nonempty members in this example (29 if the unique empty decomposition of empty is included).

INFINITELY MANY COUNTEREXAMPLES
For k>=0 add k new independent coordinates Z, and form
 F_k={A union T:A in F, T subset Z}.
Then |F_k|=18*2^k and f_Fk(b)=8*2^k, still below half.
Its irreducible generators are the six original ones plus the new singleton coordinates. Each minimal decomposition consists of an old minimal decomposition (possibly empty) and exactly the selected new singletons. Thus each old coordinate's score is multiplied by2^k. For k>=1 each new coordinate has score29*2^(k-1), whereas b has19*2^k=38*2^(k-1). Therefore b remains the unique highest-scoring coordinate for every k. This argument, rather than a finite test alone, establishes an unbounded family of failures.

VERIFICATION
The search computed minimality through private-coordinate tests. A second checker independently enumerated combinations of generators and tested minimality by explicitly removing each generator and recomputing the union. It checked exact closure, that the displayed generators are precisely the irreducibles, generation of the entire family, all scores, all frequencies, and uniqueness and deficiency of the maximizer. It enumerated28 nonempty minimal decompositions. Product formulas were additionally checked for k=1,2,3,4.
A small checker setup error (missing __file__ in a dynamically loaded definitions namespace) was corrected before verification; it did not alter the mathematical candidate or search result.

NEXT-STEP IMPLICATION
Reject this universal selector. The broader use of generators/private witnesses is not refuted. Any revised score needs an explicit argument controlling representation multiplicity; per-target normalization is a possible new hypothesis, not a demonstrated repair, and was not tested or credited here. Avoid changing the failed rule after seeing the answer and treating the altered rule as the original result.
