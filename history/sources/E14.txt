STRANGE SEED / TRUTH & WONDER
Frankl exploration 14 — balanced weights, partial blocks, and saturated images
22 October 2026 (UTC)

OUTCOME

The previous 25-coordinate uniform-weight counterexample has a balanced
certificate covering ALL permissible fibres, including fibres with partial
coordinate blocks. The example has no singleton or two-element member. Equal
total weight on two of its three-coordinate blocks works; the other coordinates
receive zero weight. This is more than a sample-only LP proposal.

There is also a self-contained hand proof for an unbounded product class.
The proof explains why the weighting works and identifies a numerical condition.
At equality, the plain graph relaxation can fail even though the weighting is
valid; a saturated-image argument repairs that gap. Beyond the condition, a
concrete permissible fibre actually defeats the same weighting.

The original modal-support claim and Frankl's conjecture remain unresolved.
The product lemma is a local derived result, not a claim of historical novelty.
No new poem was drawn, and no controlled poetry efficacy trial was performed.

BACKGROUND AND NOTATION

F is a finite union-closed family of distinct sets containing empty.
q_F(A) counts ORDERED pairs (B,C) in F squared with B union C=A, equal pairs
included. The original candidate says every maximizer A, including ties, contains
an element occurring in at least half of F. This investigation has not proved it.

Fix a modal A. Its internal core is G={B in F:B subset A}.
For each occurring nonempty exterior trace Z, let
 H_Z={B intersect A:B in F and B minus A=Z}.
Each H contains A, is union-closed and G-stable: g union h belongs to H for
every g in G and h in H. Write Q_A(K,L) for the ordered union-to-A count.
Every actual H satisfies
          C_G(H):=2Q_A(G,H)+Q_A(H,H) <= Q_A(G,G).              (1)
These are disjoint contributions to q_F(A union Z), whereas q_F(A)=Q_A(G,G).

For nonnegative coordinate weights p_i, total W>0, set
 S_p(H)=sum_{T in H}(2 sum_{i in T}p_i-W).
If S_p(G)>=0 and every permissible H has S_p(H)>=0, summing over disjoint
fibres proves an abundant coordinate within the positive weight support.
Permissible means satisfying the necessary conditions above; different such
fibres need not coexist globally, so a failure in this relaxed class need not
be a counterexample to the original modal claim.

For T in H, the principal image I_T={g union T:g in G} is contained in H.
Hence T is forbidden if C_G(I_T)>Q_A(G,G). The ordinary graph method keeps
these forbidden traces and unary implications T -> T union g, but drops binary
union closure and the full nonlinear bound (1). A negative graph optimum can
therefore be a false alarm about the weighting's true fibre validity.

PRODUCT LEMMA — PRECISE STATEMENT

Let B,D,E be pairwise disjoint finite coordinate blocks, with D,E nonempty.
Let K subset 2^B be union-closed, containing empty and B. Put r=|K|,
q=Q_B(K,K), and
 G={k union d union e:k in K, d in {empty,D}, e in {empty,E}},
 A=B union D union E.
Suppose G is EXACTLY the internal core of A in F, and A maximizes q_F.
If
                         q <= 2r+1,                          (2)
then the coordinate weights
 p_i=1/(2|D|) for i in D,
 p_i=1/(2|E|) for i in E,
 p_i=0 for i in B
have nonnegative surplus in G and in every permissible fibre H.
Consequently some coordinate of D union E occurs in at least half of F.

If q<2r+1 and F has any member outside A, this abundance is STRICT. At equality
the proof guarantees at least half, and does not in general guarantee strictness.
The lemma is conditional on the exact internal core and modal target; containing
some related product subfamily alone is not the same hypothesis.

HAND PROOF, INCLUDING ALL PARTIAL BLOCKS

The core has 4r members and Q_A(G,G)=9q. Every coordinate of D or E occurs in
exactly half the core, so its balanced surplus is zero.

Take any trace T in a permissible H. If T contains D wholly or E wholly, its
balanced weight is at least 1/2 and its individual surplus coefficient is
nonnegative. This observation does not assume the rest of T consists of whole
blocks; every possible partial subset of D,E or B is allowed.

It remains to consider a T that contains neither D nor E wholly. Let
 T_B=T intersect B, T_D=T intersect D, T_E=T intersect E,
 I_B={k union T_B:k in K}, u=Q_B(K,I_B), v=Q_B(I_B,I_B).
Then I_T is the product of I_B with {T_D,D} and {T_E,E}. Because T_D and T_E
are proper subsets, each two-state factor has ordered cross-union count 3,
both against {empty,full block} and against itself. Thus
                   C_G(I_T)=9(2u+v).                        (3)
B belongs to I_B, so u>=r and v>=1. Therefore
                   C_G(I_T)>=9(2r+1).                       (4)
If q<2r+1, this exceeds the modal budget 9q. Such a T cannot occur. Every
trace in every H consequently has nonnegative coefficient. Since A belongs
to H and has coefficient 1, each exterior fibre has surplus at least 1.
Together with the zero core surplus, this proves the strict case.

At q=2r+1, a bad T can occur only at equality throughout (3)-(4). In particular
v=1. Since B belongs to I_B, v>=2|I_B|-1, forcing I_B={B}. As empty is in K,
T_B belongs to I_B, so T_B=B. Moreover C_G(I_T)=9q exactly.

If H contained any trace outside I_T, that additional trace would strictly increase
the cost: union with the full member A of G contributes at least one additional
cross pair, and union with A in I_T contributes two self-family ordered pairs.
Hence the cost increases by at least four. Thus H=I_T, a family of four traces.
Every coordinate of D or E occurs in at least two of those four traces, so its
balanced surplus is nonnegative. This handles all equality cases with a bad T.
Fibres with no bad T were already handled pointwise. Summing completes the proof.

BALANCED REPAIR OF THE PREVIOUS NO-SMALL-SETS EXAMPLE

Take disjoint blocks P,Q,R,D,E of sizes (3,3,13,3,3), with
 K={empty,P,Q,P union Q,P union Q union R}.
Here r=5, q=9<11, |G|=20 and |A|=25. The minimum size of a nonempty core member
is 3. Integer weights 1 on each of the six coordinates of D union E, 0 elsewhere,
are a scaled version of the lemma's balanced weights.

The exact graph-relaxation minimum is 6; core surplus is 0. The ordinary
uniform-on-all-25 weighting has relaxed minimum -45,764 and core surplus -132.
The previous explicit five-set fibre H={k union D union E:k in K} has uniform
surplus -3 and modal cost 81, but balanced integer surplus +30. This is an
actual repair of that failure, not merely a different graph output.

Five representative single-coordinate weightings, one per core-signature block,
all have negative graph minima. Within each signature block, the coordinates
are symmetric, so every individual-coordinate weighting fails THIS graph test.
This does not establish that each has an actual negative permissible fibre;
those negative minima may exploit the relaxation's missing constraints.

An unequal-block check uses sizes (3,3,13,3,4), |A|=26. Use per-coordinate integer
weights 4 on D and 3 on E, equalizing total weight 12 on each block. Its exact
graph minimum is 24, core surplus 0. Uniform weights again fail. Equal block
TOTALS, rather than equal per-coordinate weights, express the proof.

EXACT SYMMETRY QUOTIENT — NO INDEPENDENT-PARTIAL-TRACE OMISSION

A naive graph has one vertex for every trace T subset A: 2^25=33,554,432 vertices
in the larger example. Group coordinates by identical membership signatures in
G AND identical coordinate weight. Permuting coordinates within a group
preserves G, exclusions, the unary closure implications, and the objective.

For groups of sizes b_j, a trace orbit is its vector k_j=|T intersect block_j|.
It represents m(k)=product_j binomial(b_j,k_j) distinct traces. Its objective
coefficient is
 m(k) times [2 sum_j p_j*k_j - W],
where p_j is the PER-COORDINATE weight in the group. Union with g either fills
an entire group or leaves its count unchanged. The quotient adds exactly those
implications. Forbidden status is computed from a representative's actual
principal image; the separate verifier recomputes it using the actual bitmask.
For whole-group cores, image collisions and union-to-top counts depend only on
which groups are already wholly present. Partial counts still affect orbit
multiplicity and the weighted objective and are never discarded.

Why this gives the EXACT minimum of the unary relaxation: its feasible trace
families are closed under union and intersection. The objective is modular, so
the union and intersection of two optimal families are also optimal. The union
of all symmetry translates of an optimum is therefore an invariant optimum.
Invariant families correspond exactly to unions of complete orbits. The quotient
graph represents those families with their full multiplicities. This argument
applies to the unary relaxation, not to the original nonlinear feasible class:
unions of actual permissible fibres need not preserve the modal budget.

The 25-coordinate balanced graph has 3,584 trace-orbit vertices and represents
ALL 33,554,432 traces. The 26-coordinate graph has 4,480 orbit vertices and
represents ALL 67,108,864 traces. Unequal point weights split a signature block
further so the invariance argument remains valid.

WHEN GRAPH FAILURE IS A RELAXATION GAP: SATURATED IMAGES

For ANY core, if C_G(I_T)=q_G and T occurs in a permissible H, the strict
monotonicity argument in the hand proof gives H=I_T. This yields an exact split:
 (a) Check every saturated principal image I_T as a complete candidate fibre.
 (b) For all remaining fibres exclude saturated traces as well as over-budget
     traces, and run the graph relaxation on this residual class.
If every saturated image has nonnegative surplus and the residual minimum is
nonnegative, the weighting is certified for all permissible fibres. If the full
trace A is itself saturated, all fibres are the singleton {A}; the current graph
implementation assumes the residual class still contains A and would instead
need that case handled directly. Current split tests satisfy this assumption.

Symmetry reduces checking saturated images to one representative per trace
orbit. Its weighted score is unchanged by the within-group permutations. Each
actual representative image, exact cost and score are exported for verification.

Concrete equality case: K=2^{a,b}, r=4, q=9=2r+1, and |D|=|E|=3. Here |A|=8.
The ordinary balanced graph minimum is -30. It does not disprove the weighting:
the saturated-image split checks image scores with minimum 0, and its residual
graph has minimum +6. The weighting is valid, as the hand proof predicts.

Thus this session distinguishes both kinds of failure: a bad weighting can
produce a real negative permissible fibre, while a valid weighting can produce
a negative graph optimum because the relaxation omits joint cost constraints.

OUTSIDE THE LEMMA CONDITION: A REAL NEGATIVE FIBRE

Let B have three singleton coordinates, K=2^B, r=8, q=27>17=2r+1.
Let |D|=|E|=3 and choose one coordinate d0 in D. Use the same product core G.
Define
 H={B union d union e:d in {empty,{d0},D}, e in {empty,E}}.
It is union-closed, G-stable, contains A, and has six members. Exact counts give
 |G|=32, Q_A(G,G)=243, C_G(H)=207, S_balanced_integer(G)=0,
                         S_balanced_integer(H)=-2.
Taking F=G union {h union {z}:h in H} gives 38 sets. Direct counting confirms
A is globally modal with q_F(A)=max q_F=243. Thus this is an actual extension,
not only a permissible-fibre relaxation witness. The same balanced weighting
fails here. Some coordinates can still be abundant; it is not a counterexample
to the original modal-support claim. It shows the lemma's numerical hypothesis
cannot simply be discarded, NOT that it is an optimal necessary condition for
all possible abundance conclusions or all possible certificates.

VALIDATION

13 saved flow/cut certificates were independently checked by reconstruction:
9 large-case certificates (two weightings on each of 25/26 coordinates, plus
five point-weight representatives on the 25-coordinate core), one small boundary
control, two ordinary/split strong boundary certificates, and one outside-
threshold certificate. Verifiers check all capacities, conservation, matching
cuts, exact objectives, orbit multiplicities, actual representative costs,
and every exported saturated image. They do not use optimization to establish
the saved certificates' optimality.

The quotient's output matched the full trace graph in 36 exact comparisons:
all four two-coordinate cores containing empty/full, three block-size pairs,
and uniform/balanced/point weights. Both encodings have separate exact checkers.

All three-coordinate cores satisfying q<2r+1 were examined: 16 strict cases,
including partial D/E traces, with no allowed bad trace. All nine q=2r+1 cases
passed the saturated-image/balanced check with D/E blocks of size three.
These finite checks validate implementations and boundary handling; the hand
proof establishes the statement for unrestricted base and block sizes.

The equality extension and saturation split were developed AFTER observing the
initial threshold controls. They are adaptive mathematical development, not a
preregistered efficacy experiment. No fresh random core draw was used.

LITERATURE, SCOPE AND NEXT STEP

A limited two-engine search did not identify an exact match for this particular
modal product lemma. That is not evidence sufficient to claim novelty. Generic
weights/cutting planes and symmetry/min-cut reductions are established tools.
Primary background inspected includes Pulaj's 2017 dissertation:
https://d-nb.info/1156012813/34
and Wang's September 2026 preprint:
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25101v1
The latter's disproof concerns admissible weights on SETS, not our coordinate
weight certificate. Its cloning discussion is related context. We did not
independently verify that paper's main result. See literature-notes.txt.

This is a structured product class, not arbitrary hard nonproduct cores. The
balanced repair and conditional lemma are meaningful local progress, while
their larger relevance is unsettled. There is no external review or formal
proof-assistant verification. Poetry's causal advantage is untested here.

Next challenge nonproduct cores with the saturated-image split. Classify
negative graph optima before abandoning a weighting: check actual permissible
images, joint modal cost, and binary union closure. Prefer a structural
obstruction or repair to another collection of uniform successes.
