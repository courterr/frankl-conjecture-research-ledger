No counterexample to the modal-restricted weighted-certificate program was found in this run. The original modal-support claim remains unresolved, and the earlier at-most-five restricted result is unaffected.

The run does establish a practical limitation of the current search: 23 provisional weight choices on six-coordinate trials passed sampled fibres but failed exhaustive checking. All affected cores admit replacement weights that pass the exhaustive check. This refutes confidence in sampled-only weight candidates, not the weighted-certificate program itself.

The next checkpoint session should implement counterexample-guided fibre separation on a small set of harder cores. Do not simply increase the number of random core trials. Two sessions remain before the agreed review of whether this route deserves continued investment.

1. THE MATHEMATICAL QUESTION
For a union-closed family F containing the empty set, q_F(A) counts ordered pairs of members with union A. A modal target maximizes this count, ties included. The main claim under investigation says that every modal target contains a coordinate appearing in at least half of F.

For a fixed target A, let G={B in F:B subset of A}. An exterior trace E defines a nonempty union-closed family H_E on A. It contains A and is stable under union with G. Write Q(K,L) for the number of pairs from K x L whose union is A. Modality imposes

 2 Q(G,H_E)+Q(H_E,H_E)<=Q(G,G).                      (1)

A permissible fibre is any trace family satisfying those conditions. It may not be simultaneously realizable with other permissible fibres in a global family.

The sufficient local certificate program seeks nonnegative weights w_i summing to one, such that

 sum_i w_i(2f_G(i)-|G|)>=0,
 sum_i w_i(2f_H(i)-|H|)>=0 for every permissible H.   (2)

Adding (2) over the disjoint actual fibres and the core yields an abundant coordinate inside A. A negative nonnegative mixture of the surplus vectors can refute the certificate program for a core. It would not automatically refute the main claim, because fibre coexistence and multiplicities remain constrained.

2. BOUNDED SEARCH: 4,000 TRIALS
The search was frozen at no more than 4,000 generated core trials. It completed that budget, with 1,000 trials on each of six, seven, eight, and nine coordinates. There were 3,927 distinct labelled cores; repeated cores were not counted as independent discoveries.

Two generator regimes were used. One allowed small generators, limiting three-element generator draws after the first three draws. The other used generators of size at least floor(n/2)+1. The latter regime occupied three repeated allocation buckets; those labels do not represent three additional mathematical mechanisms. Every core contains the empty and full target, with no singleton or pair.

For each core the search tested:
 - every fibre generated by one trace seed;
 - 128 reproducible additional draws from two seeds, or from three seeds on every fourth draw.

Closure includes unions within the fibre and unions with G. A one-seed fibre is {g union T:g in G}. For several seeds, take these images over all nonempty unions of seeds. Only fibres satisfying (1) are retained.

There were 1,472,000 fibre draws and 367,221 retained draws, including duplicates. This is not exhaustive fibre coverage on the larger targets and is not uniform sampling of cores or fibres.

Outcomes over the tested fibres:
 - 3,491 trials admitted a sampled single-coordinate choice.
 - 509 needed a sampled weighted choice.
 - No exact negative mixture satisfying the modal-restricted conditions was found.

Linear programming proposed weight vectors. Rational reconstruction was followed by exact checks of nonnegativity, total weight one, and all sampled inequalities. These checks certify the supplied finite constraints only. They say nothing about fibres the sampler did not generate.

3. COVERAGE AUDIT AND AN ELEMENTARY SIZE CAP
Let r=|G|, h=|H|, and q=Q(G,G). Both G and H contain A. The pairs (g,A) for g in G and (A,t) for t in H overlap only at (A,A). Therefore

 Q(G,H)>=r+h-1.

Also Q(H,H)>=2h-1. Condition (1) consequently gives

 q>=2r+4h-3,
 h<=floor((q-2r+3)/4).                              (3)

This elementary bound is dimension-independent. No historical novelty is claimed for it.

In particular:
 - If q<2r+1, no exterior fibre is permissible at all.
 - If q<=2r+7, every permissible fibre has at most two members. Since it contains A, each coordinate occurs in at least half of that fibre. In this case any abundant core coordinate automatically transfers through all exterior fibres.

The audit found 418 trials with no possible exterior fibre. This is exact, not just absence from the sampled fibres: the sampler always includes H={A}, and its cost is the minimum 2r+1. Another 232 trials had a nonempty permissible-fibre possibility but a size cap of at most two. Thus 650 trials had this especially easy fibre structure.

A separate exact check found 94 trials whose full target did not even maximize pair counts inside G. Such a core cannot arise under a modal target of a larger family, since its internal pair counts already contradict modality. These audit categories can overlap; their counts should not be added as disjoint groups.

The passing totals therefore include structurally easy and, in some cases, impossible-modal situations. They should not be presented as 4,000 equally demanding successes.

4. EXHAUSTIVE REFINEMENT OF THE SIX-COORDINATE SUBSET
The 1,000 six-coordinate trials contained 959 distinct labelled cores. For those same cores, the full permissible-fibre enumerator from Exploration 10 was run, rather than drawing more cores.

It enumerated 81,711 core/fibre occurrences in total. Every one of the 959 cores admits weights verified exactly against all its enumerated permissible fibres:
 - 928 have a single-coordinate certificate;
 - 31 need a weighted certificate in this local program;
 - zero weighted obstructions were found.

Seventeen of these cores also occurred in Exploration 10's sample; 942 did not. This is still selected finite coverage, not a classification of all six-coordinate cores. The sample-wide enumeration uses the established C++ closure implementation; one selected case is rerun with an independent binary enumerator below.

5. CALIBRATION: 23 PROVISIONAL CHOICES FAIL
For each of the original 1,000 six-coordinate trials, take its actual provisional choice: the first sampled common coordinate, or the rational weights returned when no sampled common coordinate existed. Check that same choice against the complete fibre list for its core.

Twenty-three provisional choices violate an omitted fibre inequality. These are trial counts, so duplicate cores may appear. None is a failure of the full certificate program: exhaustive checking supplies valid replacement weights for the affected cores.

The original provisional choices were explicitly labelled as sampled-only. This calibration does not retract a proved theorem. It measures the sampling gap and explains why more passing samples cannot stand in for a separation check.

6. A CONCRETE MISSED FIBRE
Use coordinates a,b,c,d,e,f as bits 0 through 5, and A=63. In trial 1628, the core was

 G={0,15,23,27,29,30,31,39,43,45,46,47,53,55,57,59,60,61,62,63}.

It has 20 members and coordinate frequencies
 [15,13,15,15,13,13].

The sampled-only choice put all weight on a. The omitted permissible fibre was

 H={50,54,55,58,59,62,63},

with frequencies
 [3,7,4,4,7,7].

Thus a occurs in only 3 of 7 members, and its doubled surplus is -1. Exact pair counts are

 Q(G,G)=219,
 Q(G,H)=94,
 Q(H,H)=23,
 2Q(G,H)+Q(H,H)=211<=219.

This fibre is union-closed and G-stable. It is generated by the three trace seeds 50,54,58 under the allowed closure operations. Each of those seeds is indispensable: none can be generated from all the other members of H. The sampler allowed three-seed draws, but it did not draw a combination producing this particular fibre.

The missed fibre is therefore within the kind of object the sampler intended to explore. Sparse seed draws provided incomplete coverage even at six coordinates.

For this core, an independent inclusion/exclusion enumerator finds exactly the same SET of 372 permissible fibre masks as the C++ enumerator. Every coordinate fails on some permitted fibre, so no single coordinate certifies the core. However, weight 1/2 on a and 1/2 on b certifies G and all 372 fibres:

 f_G(a)+f_G(b)>=|G|,
 f_H(a)+f_H(b)>=|H| for every permissible H.

The independent checker visits 4,775 binary decision nodes and verifies the repaired certificate with integer arithmetic. Hence every actual global extension with this core and modal A has at least one of a,b globally abundant. This is a core-specific result, not a theorem for all six-coordinate cores.

The global realization G union {z union T:T in the displayed H}, using a new outside coordinate z, has 27 members and A as its unique modal target with count 219. It does not refute the main claim.

7. DOES THE MODAL RESTRICTION ACTUALLY MATTER?
An uncapped audit was planned for up to 64 eligible distinct cores, ordered from smaller ones. It stopped after its first core produced an exact obstruction to the UNRESTRICTED weighting program:

 G={0,29,43,63}, with Q(G,G)=9.

Four union-closed G-stable trace families, generated by cube extensions, have coefficients 3/11,3/11,5/22,5/22. Their averaged doubled surplus is -21/11 in EVERY coordinate. The families and coefficients are recorded in uncapped-audit.json and independently verified. Since a convex mixture is negative in every coordinate, no nonnegative coordinate weighting can certify all four families.

But their pair costs are respectively 141,141,387,387, all far above 9. They are not permissible fibres under (1). In fact, (3) allows only a singleton fibre {A} for this small core. Thus this is not the weighted obstruction sought in the main experiment. It confirms that the modal restriction changes the certificate problem, while also illustrating a particularly easy restricted case.

We audited ONE such core before stopping, not all 64. The unrestricted obstruction is an example of a standard type of weighted-certificate failure, not a historical novelty claim.

8. WHAT THIS SESSION ESTABLISHES
Established:
 - No modal-restricted weighted obstruction in the stated search.
 - Complete finite certificates for the selected 959 six-coordinate cores.
 - An explicit failure of sampled-only weight selection, with an independently verified repair.
 - A dimension-independent fibre-size cap explaining several easy cases.
 - An exact uncapped obstruction that is excluded by the modal bound.

Not established:
 - weights valid for unsampled seven-to-nine-coordinate fibres;
 - a theorem for every six-coordinate core;
 - a general structural weighting rule;
 - a solution to the modal-support claim or Frankl's conjecture;
 - comparative Strange Seed efficacy or historical novelty.

This is the first session of the agreed three-session review. It provides a concrete methodological limitation and a bounded next task, not a reason to expand random sampling indefinitely.

9. NEXT CHECKPOINT SESSION
Implement counterexample-guided separation for a few selected harder cores:
 1. Propose rational weights from existing constraints.
 2. Search directly for a G-stable, union-closed H satisfying (1) with negative weighted surplus.
 3. If one is found, verify it exactly and add it as a constraint.
 4. Repeat until weights are genuinely verified, an exact negative-mixture obstruction emerges, or the budget ends unresolved.

A timeout or failure to find a violating fibre must remain unresolved, not a pass. A solver's infeasibility message alone is not an exact certificate. No new large batch of core trials should precede this improvement.

After two more sessions, apply the agreed checkpoint: continue only for a stronger theorem, an informative counterexample, or a clearer mathematical mechanism. More passing examples alone are insufficient.
