WHY THE SEEDED EXAMPLE WORKS
For each coordinate, its global frequency is 21/29, well above half. Nevertheless, a seven-member upward-closed subfamily has four members omitting that coordinate and only three containing it. An inclusion-respecting matching must keep those four sources inside that subfamily, so three available targets cannot suffice. Thus global abundance does not supply the stronger local capacity constraints needed for this certificate.
The construction uses complements of selected subsets of the seven-point projective plane. It moves beyond the checkpoint's triple-member examples: its smallest family members have size four. The route being tested (inclusion matching) was already a baseline suggestion; the finite geometry construction and refutation were additions within this run.

MATHEMATICAL REVIEW
Assessment was parent review with condition labels visible, supported by post-response computation. It was not independent blinded adjudication. Literature novelty remains unassessed for every claimed development.
A1: the restricted theorem is valid. When X belongs to F, all occurring traces are in H_X. If H_X is not full, some singleton is absent; the other two coordinates hit every nonempty trace. The full trace contributes +2 and an empty trace at worst -2 to that pair's balance in each fibre, proving an abundant member of the pair. The unqualified opening claim still has a gap and is not credited as proved.
A2: valid. The 19-member construction is closed under union, has frequencies (10,10,6,14,14,14,14), and the two stated relatively upward-closed witnesses each have 11 members with only 5 containing the relevant abundant coordinate. It does not refute global existence of a matching coordinate; the worker correctly left that unresolved.
B1: valid. If a nonempty proper subset of S is present, the known one-/two-element theorem supplies the bound. Otherwise the empty-outside fibre is nonnegative and uses at least one member, so 3b<=m-1. The balance identity gives the strict bound. Tracking the empty-outside fibre more closely proves the two conditional inequalities.
B2: valid. Pairwise unions of distinct co-singleton outside parts go to X, where a full trace cube is provided. Counts give m=9q+9 and each chosen-triple frequency 4q+5=4m/9+1. This proves sharpness of the leading coefficient and infinitely many exact integer cases. Outside coordinates remain abundant, so these are not counterexamples to Frankl.
C1: valid. In the cyclic construction, the seven triples form the seven-point projective plane. Cross-line intersections have size at most one, and same-line nonsingleton intersections retain the designated point. The complement family is therefore union-closed. Direct counting gives 29 members, frequency 21 everywhere, and a 4-versus-3 upward-set obstruction for every coordinate.
C2: valid. The local obstruction bounds every matching by 7, and the seven explicit complement matches are distinct and valid. Computational maximum matchings independently have size 7 on all seven coordinates.
C3: valid. The point-line incidence graph of a projective plane is regular and balanced, so Hall's edge-counting argument gives distinct incident centers for all lines. The complement family has m=1+(q^2+q+1)2^q members. Each point's complement frequency is (q+2)2^(q-1), while its local deficit is 2^q-q-1. Prime orders supply an infinite sequence. This proves the asymptotic statements and the obstruction to a fixed unmatched-source allowance.
