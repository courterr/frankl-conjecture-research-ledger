PRECISE QUESTION TESTED
Let F be finite and union-closed, S a three-element member, and Y the union of members disjoint from S. Let G={A in F:A subset S union Y}, H=F minus G, I=|G| and O=|H|. Let
 K={y in Y:2f_G(y)>=I}.
The survivor claim P is:
 If all three elements of S are globally below half and K is nonempty, then some y in K has 2f_F(y)>=|F|.
A counterexample must make every core-abundant Y candidate globally deficient while retaining global deficiency of all S elements. It may still have abundant Y elements that were not core-abundant, or abundant elements outside S union Y. Therefore it need not contradict Frankl.

Do not confuse P with the different statement Q that global deficiency of S always forces some globally abundant element somewhere in Y. P makes a claim about survival of the particular core candidates; Q allows new abundant Y elements. The computational runs below tested failures of P, not necessarily failures of Q.

The ground set in each model consists of S, k anchor coordinates Y, and one exterior coordinate z. Every subset has a binary inclusion variable. Constraints enforce union-closure; membership of S and all eight Y union R for R subset S; absence of exterior members disjoint from S; core abundance of exactly a prescribed r-element K; core deficiency of every other Y coordinate; and global strict deficiency of S union K. This makes Y the actual disjoint-member anchor and G exactly the z-free slice. The full-cube condition is a previously proved necessary condition in the all-S-deficient branch.

The model only covers one exterior coordinate and the specified k,r values. It does not cover larger anchor sizes, more exterior coordinates, other candidate counts, or arbitrary set families.

ANALYTIC ATTEMPT
For y in K, write g_y=2f_G(y)-I>=0 and let M_y count exterior members omitting y. Exact bookkeeping gives
 2f_F(y)-|F|=g_y+O-2M_y.
Simultaneous dilution requires
 2M_y>g_y+O for every y in K,
and hence
 2 sum over y in K of M_y>sum over y in K of g_y+|K|O.
This makes the required omission budget explicit. It does not contradict union-closure without a further structural upper bound on those simultaneous omissions. The depth-at-most-two theorem from Exploration02 supplies such a bound in a restricted case; its depth-three counterexample shows that it cannot simply be extended unchanged. That counterexample has abundant S-elements, so it does not answer P.

Trying to derive the missing bound using only the relation between G and H leads to a known transfer framework. Both G and H are union-closed, and G vee H subset H, where vee means all pairwise set unions. If every S element is deficient, the Exploration01 localization result gives I>9|F|/16, hence I>O. But a size advantage and compatibility alone did not yield a proved common-abundance statement in this pass.

A useful guard against vacuous tiny cases: if |Y|<=3 and F has no singleton or pair member, there are at most two disjoint-from-S members (empty and possibly the full three-element Y). The earlier disjoint-member/fibre balance argument already yields abundance in S. If a singleton or pair is present instead, standard small-set arguments yield a globally abundant element. This guard does not prove P in general, and was not used as a substitute for the tested k=4 and5 cases.

LITERATURE FINDING
Tom Eccles posed a closely related common-abundance conjecture in a Polymath11 comment on February11,2016,7:42am. It asks whether two nonempty union-closed families A,B, with A vee B subset B and |A|>|B|, must share an abundant element. This is an old transfer direction, not an approach first introduced here.
https://gowers.wordpress.com/2016/02/08/func2-more-examples/
Relevant passage: the comment containing Conjecture1 and Conjecture2, following the February10 discussion of coordinate-slice induction. Gowers's surrounding discussion explicitly emphasizes preserving the mixed-union compatibility between slices.

Our inference: if that general common-abundance assertion held for G,H here, a common abundant element would be globally abundant. It cannot lie outside S union Y because it is core-abundant, and cannot lie in S because those are assumed globally deficient. It would therefore lie in K and establish P. This explains the connection; it is not a proof, equivalence, or claim that the literature has resolved P. We did not establish the current status of Eccles's weaker common-abundance conjecture.

A stronger assertion, that every abundant element of the larger family stays abundant in the smaller compatible family, was explicitly refuted by Eccles on February24,2016,9:57pm:
https://gowers.wordpress.com/2016/02/22/func4-further-variants/
His example uses
 A={{1,2,3} union T:T subset {4,5,6}}, |A|=8,
 B={{4,5,6} union T:T subset {1,2}} union {{1,2,3,4,5,6}}, |B|=5.
Every cross union is the full ground set, which is in B. Coordinate3 has frequency8 in A and1 in B. Some coordinates remain abundant in both, so this does not refute the weaker common-abundance statement or P.

The broad framework is also covered by Poonen's classic local-configuration theorem, although its exact hypotheses and conclusion differ from P:
B. Poonen, Union-closed families, JCTA59(2),1992,253–268.
https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(92)90068-6
This source was checked at its abstract for the general local-configuration scope; no additional theorem was imported without verification.
