MATHEMATICAL REVIEW
Parent assessment with condition labels visible, supported by post-response computational checks. No independent blinded adjudication or literature novelty audit was performed.

Common new criterion: valid. For a pair P=S minus {c}, a fibre's pair-balance is 2(1-e-t_c+t_P), where e,t_c,t_P indicate its empty trace, the omitted singleton, and the pair P. If no family member is disjoint from S, e=0 and every pair-balance is nonnegative. Otherwise their union Y belongs to F. If {c} is missing in H_Y, a fibre containing an empty trace cannot also contain {c}, since its outside part is contained in Y and unioning with Y would contradict the missing singleton. All pair-balances are then nonnegative, giving an abundant element of P. When all triple elements are below half, every singleton must occur at Y, hence H_Y is full. This proves both B1 and C1.

Common terminal-fibre refutation: valid. A new greatest member containing a fresh coordinate preserves union-closure and contributes exactly one occurrence to each original coordinate. Starting from a triple-deficient example with sufficient margin keeps the triple deficient, but the only member at the enlarged outside-universe fibre has trace S. The examples have sizes/frequencies 37/18 (A,C) and 35/17 (B). Outside coordinates remain abundant. These are not counterexamples to Frankl.

A's general terminal-pattern construction: valid. A nonempty upward-closed H on S is closed under union with any old trace. Appending the corresponding new outside layer therefore preserves closure. With h=|H| and q>=h+2, the calculated gap m'-2f'(x)=q-1+h-2h_x is positive for each x in S. This covers every such H, including the full cube. It does not classify all possible non-upward terminal patterns.

A's exact projective-plane matching: valid with the source partition made explicit. The singleton {x} is assigned only to its own centered line L_x. On each other incident line, use the sources generated by that line, which contain both x and its assigned center. Removing x gives a valid target retaining that center. These nonempty targets are distinct across lines and avoid the separately matched empty/singleton targets on L_x. The matching size is q*2^(q-1)+q+1, attaining the earlier Hall upper bound.
The response's phrase 'source supported on this line' is read in that generating-line sense; treating the singleton {x} as a source of every incident line would duplicate it. The verifier initially made that duplication, then was corrected to require the nonassigned line's center. This is a validation-code correction/partition clarification, not an additional scored worker proposal. The completed matching was checked explicitly.
