CANDIDATE AND PRECISE RESTRICTED STATEMENT

F is a finite union-closed family of distinct sets, containing empty and at
least one nonempty member. Let e_F(A) be the number of UNORDERED pairs {B,C}
of proper members below A whose union is A. Such members are distinct and
incomparable. With q_F(A) the ordered union-to-A count and r_F(A) the number
of family members inside A,
                    e_F(A)=(q_F(A)-2r_F(A)+1)/2.             (1)

General candidate: if max e_F>0, EVERY maximizing A contains an element in at
least half of the GLOBAL family F. This general statement remains unresolved.

Restricted result certified here: the conclusion holds whenever |A|<=5,
regardless of the total number of coordinates or members of F.
At max e=0, F is a chain and its least nonempty member already supplies an
abundant coordinate. The zero case needs no enumeration.

CORRECT NEW FIBRE BOUND — THE OLD LISTS ARE INSUFFICIENT

Fix A. Its exact internal core is G={B in F:B subset A}, with r=|G| and
q=Q_A(G,G), where Q_A(K,L) counts ordered cross pairs with union A.
For an occurring nonempty exterior trace Z, put
 H={B intersect A:B in F, B minus A=Z}, h=|H|.
H contains A, is union-closed and G-stable: g union T in H for every g in G,
T in H. Define C_G(H)=2Q_A(G,H)+Q_A(H,H).

Inside the subfamily G union {T union Z:T in H}, the target D=A union Z has
ordered union count C_G(H) and r+h members below it. Thus its proper-pair
count is [C_G(H)-2(r+h)+1]/2. The full family's e_F(D) is at least this large.
Because e_F(A)=[q-2r+1]/2 is maximal, every actual fibre must obey
                         C_G(H)-2h <= q.                    (2)

This is WEAKER than the old q-mode condition C_G(H)<=q. Therefore the old
q-mode fibre certificates could not simply be reused. Across the same 119
core classes, the old cap admits 1,483 fibres, whereas (2) admits 2,178:
695 additional fibre occurrences. All old-cap fibres lie in the new lists.
Of the relevant 111 core classes, the count increases from 1,481 to 2,166.

The adjusted cost is monotone on families containing A. Adding a new trace
adds at least one cross pair with the full core member A, two self-family
ordered pairs with A, and subtracts 2 from the adjustment. Its net increase
is at least 2. This justifies pruning by (2) during closure enumeration.

WHY THIS ALSO COVERS SMALLER TARGETS

One may use exploration 17's hand proof directly. Alternatively, the present
result is self-contained via padding. For a nonempty maximizing A with |A|<5,
replace one of its coordinates by enough perfectly correlated copies to make
the target size five, applying the same injective union-preserving map to the
ENTIRE actual family F. All inclusion relations, union decompositions, scores,
mode ties and coordinate frequencies are preserved (copies inherit the old
coordinate's frequency). Applying the five-coordinate result and mapping back
gives an abundant coordinate in the old A. This is not a claim that arbitrary
future fibres preserve clone blocks; it is an embedding of one whole actual
family into a class already covered by the theorem.

INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION

The C++ enumerator uses canonical closure extension: from a G-stable union-
closed H, adding T forces I_T and I_(h union T) for h in H. Canonical earlier-
trace rejection prevents duplicates. It prunes only by the monotone adjusted
cost. It exports every fibre bitmask and an explicit completion flag.

A separate Python implementation makes binary include/exclude decisions over
all 32 possible traces, using iterative queue-style closure and the adjusted
bound. It used 27,499 decision nodes and produced EXACTLY THE SAME FIBRE SET
for every one of the 119 classes. Duplicates, omissions relative to the other
enumerator, or incomplete C++ runs cause verification failure.

The verifier independently checks classifications, each certificate and all

The one-exterior-fibre counterexample check found no actual counterexample.
This check is secondary; sufficiency follows from the complete fibre

Not every permitted H need produce a globally modal extension by itself; that
