CORE QUESTION
In a finite union-closed family containing the empty set, let e_F(A) count
unordered proper pairs whose union is A. Does every positive global e-maximizer
contain an abundant coordinate? This question remains unresolved in the project.
It is not a proof of Frankl's conjecture; exact literature status is unconfirmed.

MATHEMATICAL CHECKPOINT
Seven coordinates, indexed 0..6, top mask127. The source core G37 has q597 and
top score262. Adjoining mask100 gives G38, q613 and top score269. Exact finite
certificates show that 17 of 21 pair supports are blocked. Four remain:
(1,4), (3,4), (3,5), (5,6), each requiring equal weights.
On G37, the minimum cost of a negative equal-pair fibre for (4,6) is exactly609.
Four other certificates survive every union-closed core extension of G37 with
q<=629. Reaching q>=631 is necessary for an additive attack on that protection,
not sufficient. The goal of eliminating all one/two-coordinate supports was unmet.

The necessary fibre cap is c_G(H)=2Q(G,H)+Q(H,H)-2|H| <= q_G(A).
Do not reuse the older unadjusted cap. Failure of a local sufficient certificate
does not imply failure of abundance. Full definitions and proofs are in NOTE.md.
